PLP Startup Pitch • First Draft

Kakuma Connect

One simple place to discover local services, businesses, jobs, learning opportunities and trusted community information in Kakuma.

Founder: Barach AduotCommunity-first digital platform
Connecting people to opportunity01 / 12
02 • The problem

Useful information is scattered.

Hard to discover

People may not know which nearby businesses and services are available or how to contact them.

Missed opportunities

Job openings, training, scholarships and community announcements can be spread across many channels.

Time and trust

Searching through old posts and forwarded messages can be confusing and time-consuming.

Small businesses

Local providers may have limited ways to reach people beyond word of mouth.

Kakuma Connect02 / 12
03 • The solution

A local digital directory built around people.

Kakuma Connect brings useful local information into one mobile-friendly platform, making it easier to find services and discover opportunities.

Find

Browse local services, shops and community resources by category.

Discover

Explore jobs, training, scholarships and other opportunities.

Connect

Reach providers through contact details and links they choose to publish.

Stay informed

See dated announcements with a source or contact for follow-up.

Kakuma Connect03 / 12
04 • Product

Designed for simple access on a phone.

Home and search

Clear categories and a search field to find what matters.

Services directory

Business and service profiles with location details, opening information when provided, and contact options.

Opportunity board

Listings for jobs, courses, scholarships and events with deadlines and source links.

Community updates

Moderated notices with dates, categories and reporting options.

Initial product scope: a lightweight mobile-friendly website. A dedicated app can be considered after user testing.

Kakuma Connect04 / 12
05 • Customers and market

Start with the Kakuma community.

Primary users

Residents and young people looking for services, learning and work opportunities.

Local providers

Small businesses, service providers, training centres and community organisations.

Initial geography

Kakuma and nearby communities, with expansion only after local needs are understood.

Market validation

Interview residents and providers, test demand, and measure repeat use before estimating market size.

No market-size figures are claimed in this first draft. Validate population, reachable users and provider counts with reliable local sources.

Kakuma Connect05 / 12
06 • Business model

Keep essential discovery accessible.

Free basic listings

Core community information and basic provider profiles remain free to browse.

Promoted listings

Optional paid visibility for businesses, clearly labelled as sponsored.

Partner services

Possible service agreements with organisations that need to share verified opportunities.

Digital support

Optional profile setup or digital promotion support for local providers.

Revenue streams are hypotheses to test. No fees or contracts have been agreed at this stage.

Kakuma Connect06 / 12
07 • Competition

Bring scattered channels together.

WhatsApp and social groups

Fast sharing, but information can be difficult to search and may become outdated.

Noticeboards and word of mouth

Useful locally, but reach depends on who sees or hears the message.

General job and directory websites

Can offer broad reach, but may not focus on Kakuma-specific services and updates.

Our difference

A locally focused, organised directory with categories, dates, source links and community feedback.

These are alternative channels, not claims of a complete competitor analysis. Validate specific competitors with users.

Kakuma Connect07 / 12
08 • Go-to-market

Build trust locally, one category at a time.

1. Listen

Interview residents, youth groups and service providers about their most common information needs.

2. Pilot

Launch a small directory with a limited set of checked listings and opportunity sources.

3. Partner

Invite community organisations, learning centres and businesses to share accurate updates.

4. Improve

Use feedback, usage data and listing freshness checks to improve the platform.

10 • Financial plan

Start lean and measure the costs.

Initial cost areas

Domain and hosting, design and development tools, user research, content verification and basic support.

Operating costs

Hosting, maintenance, moderation, provider onboarding and communications.

Early income tests

Test optional promoted listings and partner service agreements only after user value is proven.

Financial discipline

Prepare a 12-month budget after collecting real supplier quotes and pilot requirements.

Detailed revenue and expense forecasts are intentionally left for validation rather than invented.
